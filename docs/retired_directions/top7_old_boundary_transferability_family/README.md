## 1. Historical Question

This family asked one old-boundary question:

> why does the legacy `top7` donor lane fail to stay replace-compatible, while `top3` remains the strongest old-boundary-compatible anchor/control?

It should not be read as redefining the current clean StepC canonical chain.

## 3. Final Conclusions

- `top3` is the strongest legacy old-boundary-compatible anchor/control; it is not a claim of global natural optimum
- `top7` widening is not falsified as learnable semantics, but the old-boundary training / handoff path fails to preserve replace-compatibility
- support scope has real causal leverage, but scope isolation alone does not recover a clean replace-compatible donor
- curriculum, leg-first, and allocation-only variants all fail to beat the legacy `top3` anchor/control
- the main incompatibility boundary is earlier than a simple replace-side absorb story
- head-input matching is ruled out; the more useful boundary reading shifts to shared-head output / downstream consumer contract

## 5. Failure Analysis

The most accurate family-level read is:

- the failure is not “top7 impossible”
- the failure is not “just one missing schedule tweak”
- the failure is not well explained by head-input distribution mismatch
- the failure is better explained as a donor-contract / boundary problem:
  - old-boundary `top7` expansion enters replace with a compromised shared contract
  - `top3` preserves the best known old-boundary-compatible anchor basin
  - simple replace-side split / absorb variants are not decisive enough to rescue the old-boundary `top7` family
